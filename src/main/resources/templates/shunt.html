About Shunt

Shunt is a conveyor-routing puzzle. Boxes arrive at the top of the board and ride the belts. You click the switches that direct them to their destinations. The browser only runs the clock; the C core owns the rules.

It's a tribute to Global Player, a mid-2000s Flash game for logistics company Dachser, where you sorted colour-coded parcels onto the right trucks, trains, and planes. Finished, Shunt will be ten levels of rising difficulty. Today it's a work in progress.

Why C

Not for speed, for the problem. I could build this in an afternoon with a good game engine, which is the reason not to. C gives you nothing for free, so rendering is never for box in boxes: box.draw(). It's plain functions reaching into plain structs and reading their state, without tangling everything up. I've learned a lot by building tributes to classic games in Java Swing, JPanels, and Processing, but this is something different. The game doesn't even have an image grid, just a flat array of hand-addressed pixels.

What's been hard

I've been caught out by little things continually: 8 bit integers overflowing, 32 bits per pixel, modulo of negative numbers. I've had to refactor things as my model and view became coupled together. These are the little things that a language without guardrails throws at you constantly, and there's nowhere to hide. Doing it the hard way, in the open, is the point.
